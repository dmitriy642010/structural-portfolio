Railway station with four concourses

Two constraints shaped the design: existing infrastructure beneath the platform and progressive collapse assessment for all four passenger concourses.

My role
Structural Engineering Team Lead
Platform structure
18 m reinforced concrete beam
Tools
Revit · LIRA-SAPR · IDEA StatiCa

Spanning over existing infrastructure

Underground utilities and an existing pedestrian underpass beneath the platform limited where new supports could be placed. We used an 18 m reinforced concrete beam to carry the platform across the constrained area without an intermediate support.

Assessing progressive collapse in all four concourses

All four concourses were classed as areas of mass occupancy, so each required a progressive collapse assessment. I used finite element models to study the structural response and load redistribution after the notional removal of selected columns and beams.

From analysis to connection design

Findings from the structural assessment informed the design of the steel connections. I detailed the connections in Revit and used IDEA StatiCa to check component behaviour.

My contribution

I worked on the structural models, designed steel connections, developed precast concrete components and coordinated the structural documentation. I also presented and substantiated engineering decisions during state technical review.

Project outcome

Documentation and material quantities
6 months
Team members I coordinated
4
Meetings with the client
Weekly

We completed the structural documentation and material quantity take-offs in Revit in six months. We handed the quantity schedules over to the cost estimating team to support procurement.

I assigned tasks and coordinated the four-person team. I met with the client weekly, and we incorporated many of the requested changes into the design.

The project passed the state design review and is now under construction.

25-storey residential complex

A 25-storey, three-section reinforced concrete residential complex with underground parking, built on challenging ground conditions. The project involved the design of 1,325 CFA piles, ranging from 18 to 27 metres in length, and advanced nonlinear structural analysis to optimise reinforcement and ensure structural performance.

Building
25 storeys · Three sections
Foundation
1,325 CFA piles
Tools
Revit · LIRA-SAPR · Navisworks

Structural analysis and excavation design

Weak ground required piled foundations across the footprint of the residential buildings and the underground car park. Excavation was one of the most demanding parts of the design. The retaining system had to be checked at successive construction stages. The building analysis also accounted for wind-induced movement and differential settlement across the three sections.

A material nonlinear analysis was carried out in LIRA-SAPR for all three sections to account for the strength and nonlinear response of reinforced concrete. The results identified where extra reinforcement was required and where it could be omitted, reducing unnecessary steel use.

Reinforcement detailing in Revit

The Revit models show how reinforcement fits together at wall pier-to-slab and pile-to-foundation slab junctions, as well as around the crane foundation. Modelling these interfaces in 3D made it possible to check how bars from adjoining elements fit together before completing the drawings.

Construction drawings

The drawing package documented the structural layout, member geometry and reinforcement through plans, elevations, sections, details and bar schedules.

Project outcome

Reinforced concrete CFA piles
1,325
Design team members
5
Design period
7 months

The residential complex has been built. The CFA piles were 650 mm in diameter and ranged from 18 to 27 m in length. They were installed across the footprint of the residential buildings and the underground car park.

Over seven months, my five-person team carried out the structural design and prepared the construction drawings. We used a federated model in Navisworks to detect and resolve clashes with other disciplines, coordinating dense building services in the car park and their routes through the residential floors up to the roof.

Residential quarter & underground parking

A group of cast-in-place reinforced concrete residential buildings above a shared underground car park. The structural design had to address groundwater across the site and accommodate factory-made balconies.

Substructure
One shared underground parking level
Off-site fabrication
Complete balcony units
Tools
Revit · LIRA-SAPR · LIRA Soil · Navisworks

Groundwater and a shared underground car park

The underground car park extends beneath the residential buildings, making groundwater a site-wide design issue. The planned drainage system collects groundwater across the development and routes it to a pond in the adjacent landscaped park.

Weak, waterlogged soils required checks for groundwater uplift, bearing capacity and differential settlement. The structural analysis model captured the interaction between the residential buildings and the car park, and the design addressed waterproofing at complex junctions.

Connections for factory-made balconies

The balconies were manufactured off site as complete units and delivered ready for installation. I developed the connection details that attach the prefabricated units to the cast-in-place concrete frame.

From the Revit load model to reinforcement design

I built the analytical model and assigned loads in Revit, then exported the model to LIRA-SAPR. I brought the calculated reinforcement requirements back into Revit to guide reinforcement layouts.

This workflow made it quicker to incorporate architectural revisions and simpler to update the affected reinforcement layouts as the design evolved.

Multidisciplinary coordination in Navisworks

I coordinated the structural model with architecture and building services in Navisworks, reviewing clashes with the other design teams across the residential blocks and in the shared underground level.

Construction progress

The construction photographs show the reinforced concrete frames, masonry infill and slab works between the residential blocks.

Residential design in a seismic region

A reinforced concrete residential development with 9- and 16-storey blocks. The design had to account for seismic intensity VII and the constraints of building far from major urban centres.

Building heights
9 and 16 storeys
Design seismic intensity
VII
Tools
LIRA-SAPR · SCAD · Revit

Seismic analysis and pile foundations

The finite element analysis considered seismic action in the relevant directions and assessed the response of the reinforced concrete frame, accounting for centres of mass, natural frequencies and frame flexibility.

Weak, waterlogged soils made the foundation design a major part of the project. The buildings were founded on 12 m precast reinforced concrete piles with a 300 × 300 mm cross-section. The pile layout was refined through analysis using a multi-row arrangement. The site’s distance from major cities added constraints to materials supply and construction logistics.

Construction drawings

The construction drawings translated the analysis into reinforcement layouts and structural details. The drawings below cover foundation slab reinforcement, typical floor slab reinforcement and detailed reinforcement at wall junctions and openings.

Project outcome

Design seismic intensity
VII
Precast reinforced concrete piles
570
Increase in reinforcement required due to seismic design
30%

The design documentation passed state expert review, and construction of the residential development was completed.
